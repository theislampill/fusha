# Staged explanation (learner-facing)

**Stage 1** — a coordinator is not a translation, it is a relation: it stands
between two things and hands the case of the first to the second.

**Stage 2** — which coordinator you are looking at depends on what the
sentence is doing: adding, ordering, offering a choice, correcting,
contrasting, or ruling the second item out.

**Stage 3** — some of these forms have twins that are not coordinators at all.
The same short word can negate a whole clause, and a doubled spelling can turn
a coordinator into a governor. Position and vowelling tell you which.

**Stage 4** — and for two members, the scholars themselves disagree about
whether they belong to the set. There the honest page shows both answers with
names attached, not one answer with confidence attached.
