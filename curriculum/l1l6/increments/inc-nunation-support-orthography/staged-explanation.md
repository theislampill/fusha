# Staged explanation (learner-facing)

**Stage 1** — the a-form of nunation is usually written with an extra alif
after it. That alif is silent; you read the ending, not the letter.

**Stage 2** — three specific endings block that extra alif: a word already
ending in the tied taa, a word already ending in a hamza that sits on an
alif, and the variant final a-spelling. In those three cases the mark just
sits on the letter that is already there.

**Stage 3** — do not stretch "already has a hamza" into the exemption. Only a
hamza sitting ON AN ALIF counts. A hamza on any other seat still needs the
extra alif, exactly like an ordinary consonant.

**Stage 4** — nunation and the definite article never appear on the same
word. If both are declared, that is not a puzzle to solve — it is a
contradiction, and the honest answer is to refuse it.
