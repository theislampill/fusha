# Reference — discriminating the أفعل skeleton

One spelling, five documented readings, three parts of speech. The skeleton
carries the elative in both its degrees, the colour-and-defect adjective, the
fourth-form perfect verb, and the first-person singular imperfect. A wrong
pick here is a category error, not a shade of meaning, so nothing in this
table is decided by the shape.

## What decides each reading

| reading | selecting evidence |
|---|---|
| elative, comparative | a comparison complement is present |
| elative, superlative | no complement, plus the definite article or an immediately following genitive |
| colour or defect adjective | no complement, and the lexeme's feminine counterpart is the colour template |
| fourth-form perfect verb | verbal entry, default person, perfect paradigm |
| first-person singular imperfect | verbal entry, first-singular prefix |

Degree is the point worth repeating: the elative has ONE form for both
degrees, and the construction fixes which is meant. The two superlative
constructions exclude the comparison complement, so a surface carrying both
is over-marked rather than doubly resolved.

The feminine counterpart is lexical evidence, not a consequence of the
reading. The elative's counterpart and the colour adjective's counterpart are
different templates, and which one a lexeme takes has to be looked up. Where
the counterpart is the colour template and the token also carries the
article, the superlative construction and the colour adjective are built
alike; both readings then survive and are preserved, attributed, with no
winner.

## What this table does not do

It does not encode the classical conditions on which bases admit an elative
at all, so a surviving elative reading is a candidate about this occurrence
and never a licence to coin one. It does not decide whether sequence and
otherness items belong to the class, a question the tradition disputes. And
it has no default: partial evidence yields an abstention, not a lean toward
the commonest reading.
