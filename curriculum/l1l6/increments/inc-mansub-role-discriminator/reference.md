# Reference — accusative role discriminator

Four roles wear one surface signature: an indefinite accusative noun standing
after a verb. Reinforcement is a verbal noun built on the verb's own root.
Motive is a verbal noun of a different root that answers a why-question, and
it holds only when agent and time coincide with the main verb and the noun is
indefinite; when a precondition fails the language uses a prepositional
construction instead. The state role is filled by an indefinite accusative
participle or adjective that answers an in-what-condition question and agrees
with a definite holder elsewhere in the clause. The respect specifier is an
indefinite accusative noun resolving a stated vagueness — a measure, a number,
an elative, a verb of increase or evaluation, or the clause as a whole — and
answers an in-what-respect question.
