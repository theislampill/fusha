# Reference — per-preposition sense tables

A preposition is not a gloss. Each of the four common genitive-assigning
prepositions covered here distributes over several senses, and the sense is
selected by the surrounding clause: the class of the governing predicate, the
semantic type of the complement, and a paraphrase test that confirms the
candidate. Case government is held constant while the semantics vary.

| preposition | senses carried here |
|---|---|
| من | starting point, constitutive material, partitive portion, explicative specification |
| ب | instrument or means, accompaniment, causal motivation, oath — plus a contested transitivising row |
| عن | departure, topic of speech or inquiry, acting in place of another |
| ل | ownership, benefit, purpose |

## What decides a sense

The governing predicate goes first. A verb of asking, speaking or knowing
selects the topic sense whatever the complement denotes, and reading physical
departure there yields an incoherent proposition. An affective predicate with
an event-denoting complement selects the causal reading, which a single fixed
English preposition would collapse into accompaniment.

The complement's semantic type narrows what remains: a place or a bound, a
substance, a mass or set, a manipulable tool, an animate participant, an
event, a goal-naming verbal noun.

The paraphrase test confirms. Where a row declares one, an unrun test is
missing evidence, not weak evidence — a substance complement under a motion
predicate is precisely the environment where guessing goes wrong, so the
table abstains there.

## Where the table deliberately does not decide

An animate complement after a verb of bringing or presenting reads either as
accompaniment or as a transitivising element. The source assigns one; the
tradition contests it. Both rows are declared, so that environment preserves
both readings.

## Boundaries

Government is invariant: every preposition here assigns genitive whichever
sense is selected, so sense selection never touches the case and case never
selects a sense. With a pronominal complement no genitive exponent is visible
at all, and the case claim there is abstract. The sense lists are the
teaching inventories, shorter than the classical ones, so a no-survivor
outcome means unlisted here rather than ill-formed. Expletive occurrences and
prepositions selected lexically by a particular verb are out of scope
entirely.
