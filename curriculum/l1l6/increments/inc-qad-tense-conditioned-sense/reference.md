# Reference — tense-conditioned sense selector

One verbal particle takes its reading from the tense class of the token
immediately after it. With a perfect verb it asserts certainty and completion;
with an imperfect verb it weakens the assertion to possibility. Each side
carries a secondary reading that sits alongside the primary one rather than
replacing it: recency on the perfect side, when the utterance is anchored at
the moment the event occurs, and frequency on the imperfect side, when the
statement is generic. The particle must be adjacent to its verb, and it
governs nothing at all — no mood and no case consequence follows from any
reading. A non-verbal token immediately after it signals that either the
analysis or the identification of the particle is mistaken.
