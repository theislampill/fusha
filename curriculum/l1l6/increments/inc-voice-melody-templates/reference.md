# Reference — voice as a melody over the radicals

Voice is carried by the vowels. The passive replaces a stem's internal vowel
melody; it adds and removes no consonant, and every template consonant of a
derived form stays in place. That is why a passive can be built for any form
in the series, and also why so few passives can be recognised by their
letters.

This unit writes each melody as a letter shape over the supplied radicals.
Two kinds of cell result.

**Cells the letters decide.** The third form's long low vowel is replaced by a
long back vowel in the perfect passive, so the two melodies are spelled
differently. Medial-weak stems have their own melody in both tenses, and it
too surfaces as a different letter. These rows are distinct in the pack, and
the unit classifies them.

**Cells the letters cannot decide.** The form-I perfect, the form-II perfect
(whose doubling is a harakat fact), the form-I imperfect, the third form's
imperfect and the fourth form's perfect all spell their active and their
passive identically. For those cells the pack deliberately declares two rows
with one shape. A surface therefore produces more than one survivor and the
unit abstains. This is the designed result: in unvocalised text the voice of
those cells is genuinely not readable from the letters, and the source that
teaches the melodies on a fully vowelled surface never says so.

Two further limits are recorded rather than papered over. The imperfect
passive melody fixes a low vowel on the middle radical, which overwrites the
lexically stored active vowel — that vowel is not recoverable from a passive
form. And the final-weak, doubly weak and geminate stem classes have no rows
here at all; a surface from those classes abstains as uncovered.

The syntactic half — the patient promoted to subject, marked nominative, and
controlling agreement — is a consequence of the voice, not a diagnostic this
unit may use. It is emitted by other units on their own evidence.
