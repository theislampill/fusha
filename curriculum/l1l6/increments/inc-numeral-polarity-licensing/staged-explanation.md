# Staged explanation (learner-facing)

**Stage 1** — one and two behave like ordinary describing words: they match
their noun and stand after it.

**Stage 2** — three to ten do the opposite. Look at the SINGULAR of the thing
counted, then choose the numeral of the other gender. Looking at the plural's
shape is the classic wrong turn.

**Stage 3** — in that band the numeral and its noun are a joined pair, so the
counted noun is plural and genitive. Its exact genitive ending depends on the
noun's own class, not on the numeral.

**Stage 4** — the numeral's own ending is a separate question entirely: it
comes from the numeral's job in the sentence. Two endings, two sources, never
copied from each other.
