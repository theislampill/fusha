# Reference — verb class by mood exponent matrix

Mood is realised differently by inflectional class, so one governor produces
several surface effects. The sound class shows a final damma with no governor,
a replacement fatha under the subjunctive governor, and a sukun under the
jussive governor. The nun-bearing forms keep their final nun with no governor
and delete it under either governed mood, with the preceding long vowel
retained; the two governed cells are therefore syncretic and cannot be told
apart from the surface. Weak-final verbs show an estimated vowel with no
governor, an overt fatha under the subjunctive, and deletion of the final weak
letter under the jussive. Hollow verbs retain the medial weak letter under the
subjunctive and delete it under the jussive, which makes that letter the
diagnostic between the two moods. The fixed-shape feminine-plural forms do not
participate in the alternation at all. The cells realised by deletion are
exactly the cells that stop mood from being read off a final vowel.
