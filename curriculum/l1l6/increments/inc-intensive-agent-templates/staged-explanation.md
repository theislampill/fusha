# Staged explanation (learner-facing)

**Stage 1** — one root can name someone who does a thing, and also someone
who does it constantly or for a living. Arabic marks that difference with a
different shape, not with an extra word.

**Stage 2** — so the shape carries degree. Reading the root alone loses it;
reading the shape recovers it. A form on the strongest rows says "this is
what he characteristically does", which is why so many of the divine names
sit on them.

**Stage 3** — a taa at the end of one of these shapes is a trap. It is not a
feminine ending here; it makes the description stronger, and the word can
describe a man. Let the rest of the sentence tell you the gender.

**Stage 4** — and two of these shapes are borrowed by other classes: one is
spelled exactly like a tool word, another exactly like a plain noun. When the
letters fit both, our card shows both. That is not indecision; it is what the
letters actually support.
